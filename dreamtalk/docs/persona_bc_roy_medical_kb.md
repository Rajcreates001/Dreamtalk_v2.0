## 4. PUBLIC HEALTH ACHIEVEMENTS

### As Mayor of Calcutta (1931–1933)
- Expanded free medical aid system
- Set up grant-in-aid framework for hospitals and charitable dispensaries
- Improved water supply and sanitation
- Better roads and lighting (public health infrastructure)
- Tabled resolution (1925) to study pollution in Hooghly River & recommend measures

### As Chief Minister (1948–1962)
- Built comprehensive public healthcare system from ground up
- Established first postgraduate medical college in Kolkata
- Founded mental hospital and infectious disease hospital
- Rural healthcare facilities across West Bengal
- Public health campaigns against malaria and cholera

### Medical Philosophy on Public Health
> "Independence from colonial rule will remain a dream until and unless the people of India are healthy and strong in mind and body."

> "Swaraj would remain a dream unless the people were healthy and strong in mind and body."

## 5. FAMOUS MEDICAL CASES

### Mahatma Gandhi — Personal Physician (1930s–1948)
- Gandhi was famously skeptical of Western medicine
- Roy earned Gandhi's complete trust over decades
- 1942: Gandhi's fast during Quit India Movement in Pune
  - Roy attended him personally
  - Convinced Gandhi to take medicines not made in India (Gandhi was boycotting British goods)
- Roy insisted on treating Gandhi free of charge
- Famous quote when Gandhi asked why he treated him for free:
  > "No Gandhiji, I could not treat all patients free. But I came not to treat Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi, but to treat 'him' who to me represents the four hundred million people of my country."

### John F. Kennedy
- Roy was consulted regarding JFK's Addison's disease and back condition
- Demonstrates his international reputation

### General Practice Approach
- Treated patients from ALL classes — rich and poor
- Never turned anyone away
- Dedicated minimum 1 hour every morning at his residence to treat underprivileged patients free of charge (continued till last day of his life)
- Known for clinical precision and extraordinary diagnostic instinct
- The British Medical Journal called him:
  > "The first medical consultant in the subcontinent"

## 7. MEDICAL EDUCATION PHILOSOPHY

Roy believed in three pillars of medical education:
1. **Clinical excellence** — hands-on training, not just theory
2. **Postgraduate specialization** — founded first PG medical college
3. **Universal access to medical education** — regardless of class/caste

### Key Contributions to Medical Education
- Transformed Calcutta Medical College into one of India's leading institutions
- Founded IPGMER — Kolkata's first postgraduate medical college
- As VC of Calcutta University (1942), maintained education during WWII:
  - Arranged air-raid shelters so classes could continue
  - Provided relief for students, teachers, and employees
  - Japanese invasion threat — Calcutta was under risk
- Helped establish 4 new universities including Jadavpur University
- Supported IIM Kolkata and IISWBM

## 11. KEY ANECDOTES FOR RAG / EMBEDDING

### The 30 Applications
> When applying to St. Bartholomew's Hospital, the dean refused to accept an Asian student. Roy submitted 30 applications until the dean finally relented. He then completed MRCP + FRCS in just 2 years 3 months.

### The Gandhi Fast (1942)
> During the Quit India Movement, Gandhi was fasting in Pune. Roy attended him and convinced him to take medicines not made in India, respecting Gandhi's boycott of British goods.

### Free Morning OPD
> Throughout his career as CM, Roy dedicated minimum 1 hour every morning at his residence to treat underprivileged patients free. He continued this until the day he died.

### The Nursing Home Will
> Roy willed ALL his properties, including his residence, to be converted into a nursing home named after his mother Aghorkamini Devi.

### The British Medical Journal Tribute
> The BMJ called him "The first medical consultant in the subcontinent."

### Math vs Medicine
> Roy graduated in Mathematics (Honors) and was accepted to Bengal Engineering College AND Calcutta Medical College. He chose medicine.
